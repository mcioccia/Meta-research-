## Filing-based enterprise-to-equity bridge check

This bridge uses only balance-sheet amounts reported in the selected 10-K. It is a reconciliation of net cash, not a market-value estimate: the 10-K does not report Meta's enterprise value or equity market value.

| Bridge item | $ millions |
|---|---:|
| Cash and cash equivalents | 35,873 |
| Add: marketable securities | 45,719 |
| **Cash and marketable securities** | **81,592** |
| Less: long-term debt at face amount | (59,000) |
| **Net cash before other claims** | **22,592** |

Check: $35,873 + $45,719 - $59,000 = $22,592 million. Therefore, using these stated items only: **equity value = enterprise value + $22,592 million**. This is not a price target or implied share value. Operating leases, non-marketable equity investments, income-tax liabilities, and other claims are excluded and require an explicit valuation policy.

## Initial thesis

**Watch / defer.** Meta’s 2025 revenue growth, ad-impression growth, and pricing growth establish a strong core advertising engine. However, the company expects $115–$135 billion of 2026 capital expenditures to support AI and the core business. I would defer initiation until research connects that spending to durable advertising revenue, margins, and returns on invested capital—and until the treatment of non-core investments, leases, and long-duration obligations is consistently defined in the valuation.

## Consequential assumptions

- Cash and marketable securities are available to equity holders and are counted dollar-for-dollar in the simple bridge.
- Debt is represented by the $59.0 billion principal amount, rather than the $58.744 billion carrying amount; the $256 million difference is immaterial to this first-pass check but should be handled consistently in a full model.
- The 2,574 million diluted weighted-average shares are evidence of dilution, but are not used to create a per-share value because the filing does not supply an enterprise value.
- The core value driver is advertising monetization: volume (ad impressions) and price per ad. Their reported 2025 growth does not by itself establish a sustainable long-run growth rate.
- The filing-based bridge excludes operating lease liabilities and non-marketable equity investments; a later valuation must explicitly decide whether and how to include both.

## Unknowns and risks to resolve

- What incremental revenue, margin, and cash return will the 2026 AI/core capex program earn, and over what useful life?
- How much of recent advertising growth is durable demand versus auction, geography, foreign-exchange, or comparison-period effects?
- Can ad load, engagement, and pricing continue to grow without degrading user/advertiser economics?
- What is the appropriate valuation treatment for Reality Labs losses, non-marketable investments, operating leases, legal/regulatory exposure, and tax liabilities?
- What share count should be used at the valuation date after repurchases, RSU issuance/vesting, and any subsequent changes?

## Research plan

1. Build a three-statement historical model from the 10-K and interim filings, separating Family of Apps and Reality Labs.
2. Decompose advertising growth into impressions, price per ad, region, and currency; compare against user and engagement measures.
3. Model capex, depreciation/useful-life assumptions, operating costs, and free cash flow under conservative/base/upside cases.
4. Reconcile an up-to-date diluted share count and capital structure, then set explicit treatment for debt, leases, cash, marketable securities, and investments.
5. Value the operating business with DCF and peer/multiple cross-checks; test the thesis against capex returns, advertising growth, and regulatory stress cases.
