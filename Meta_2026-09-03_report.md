# Meta Platforms (NASDAQ: META): MD&A update

**Prepared for FIN 43900 — September 3, 2026**  
**Current call: WATCH / DEFER**

## Executive view

Meta’s core Family of Apps is still an unusually powerful advertising business. Revenue grew from $134.9 billion in 2023 to $164.5 billion in 2024 and $201.0 billion in 2025, while 2025 operating income reached $83.3 billion. ([Meta 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 72](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm))  

However, the near-term investment question is no longer simply whether the advertising business can grow. It is whether Meta can earn an attractive return on an exceptionally large AI-infrastructure build-out without permanently lowering free-cash-flow conversion or creating a new cost base. The Q2 2026 results show both sides: revenue rose 28% year over year to $60.8 billion, but costs and expenses rose 55%, operating margin fell to 31% from 43%, and free cash flow was only $784 million after $31.1 billion of capital expenditures. ([Meta Q2 2026 results, July 29, 2026](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx))

## MD&A read: what has changed

### 1. The underlying ad business has kept compounding

Meta’s three-year revenue sequence was $134.9 billion (2023), $164.5 billion (2024), and $201.0 billion (2025). Operating income moved from $46.8 billion to $69.4 billion to $83.3 billion over the same periods. ([2025 10-K, Item 7, p. 72](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm); [2024 10-K, Item 7, p. 71](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000132680125000017/meta-20241231.htm))

In 2025, Family of Apps revenue increased 22%, almost entirely from advertising. Ad impressions rose 12% and average price per ad rose 9%; management attributed price growth mostly to ad-targeting and measurement improvements, partly offset by the lower monetization rates of some products and geographies. ([2025 10-K, Item 7, p. 73](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm))

Q2 2026 extended that momentum: Family daily active people averaged 3.60 billion, ad impressions increased 14%, and average price per ad increased 12% from a year earlier. ([Meta Q2 2026 results, July 29, 2026](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx))

### 2. AI is already supporting engagement and advertising, but its full return is unproven

On the Q2 call, CEO Mark Zuckerberg said Meta’s AI investments were improving user experiences, advertiser performance, and product-development speed; he also described possible longer-term revenue lines from APIs, business agents, and compute services. This is management’s strategy and expectation—not proof of a future revenue stream. ([Q2 2026 earnings call, July 29, 2026](https://s21.q4cdn.com/399680738/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/META-Q2-2026-Earnings-Call-Transcript.pdf))

The nearer-term evidence is more concrete. Meta’s 2025 MD&A links ad-price growth to ongoing improvements in ad targeting and measurement, and the Q2 2026 operating metrics showed simultaneous increases in impressions and price. ([2025 10-K, Item 7, p. 73](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm); [Meta Q2 2026 results, July 29, 2026](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx))

### 3. The cost and capital-intensity question has become central

In 2025 Meta spent $69.7 billion on property and equipment, largely for servers, data centers, and network infrastructure, and expected $115–135 billion of 2026 capital expenditures at the time of its 10-K. ([2025 10-K, Item 7, p. 77](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm))

By Q2 2026, the company had narrowed its expected 2026 capital-expenditure range to $130–145 billion and guided to $165–169 billion of total expenses. ([Meta Q2 2026 results, July 29, 2026](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)) Reuters reported that the range reflected Meta’s continuing data-center build-out for AI. ([Reuters, July 29, 2026](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/meta-narrows-annual-capex-forecast-as-ai-buildout-grows-4821581))

The Q2 margin compression also included $2.4 billion of legal-proceeding charges and $1.18 billion of severance expenses, so it should not be treated as entirely recurring infrastructure cost. Even after that adjustment, the $31.1 billion quarterly capital-expenditure figure makes free-cash-flow conversion the key item to monitor. ([Meta Q2 2026 results, July 29, 2026](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx))

### 4. Balance sheet capacity is real, but it is not a substitute for return discipline

As of June 30, 2026, Meta reported $90.3 billion in cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities and $83.7 billion in long-term debt. ([Meta Q2 2026 results, July 29, 2026](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)) The company can finance its program; the analytical issue is whether incremental spending produces durable ad yield, enterprise revenue, or other cash flows that justify the scale of investment.

## Key risks and limitations

- Advertising remains the economic center of the company, so weaker advertiser demand, lower engagement, or less effective targeting would matter disproportionately. The 2025 10-K identifies impressions and average price per ad as the principal drivers of advertising revenue. ([2025 10-K, Item 7, p. 73](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm))
- Reality Labs remains a material drag: its operating loss was $19.2 billion in 2025, up from $17.7 billion in 2024. ([2025 10-K, Item 7, p. 75](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm))
- Legal and regulatory uncertainty is not theoretical. Meta’s Q2 release disclosed active matters, youth-related trials, and the possibility of material losses. ([Meta Q2 2026 results, July 29, 2026](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx))
- This report does not estimate intrinsic value or set a buy price because a current market price, normalized post-build-out free cash flow, and a defensible return-on-invested-capital forecast were outside the available primary evidence.

## Current call: WATCH / DEFER

I would **watch/defer**, not initiate today. Meta has a strong, expanding ad engine and evidence that AI is helping the core product, but the 2026 spending program is large enough that I want more evidence of sustained ad-yield gains, AI-product monetization, and a recovery in free-cash-flow conversion before treating the investment as attractive.

**What would change the call:** I would reconsider an initiation if subsequent quarters show that revenue and advertising yield continue to grow while capital spending stabilizes and free cash flow rebounds without relying on one-time cost adjustments.
